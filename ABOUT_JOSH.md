# Josh, beyond the product specs

![Josh's anonymous profile avatar](assets/avatar-josh-500.png)

The main portfolio is intentionally serious: product rules, technical
architecture, validation evidence, and failure modes. This page is the part
that does not fit neatly into a requirements document.

## Making complicated things simpler

I experiment with Chinese short-form content through **简单财经** and
**简单说** on Douyin. The operating question is straightforward:

> Can finance, AI, business, history, or philosophy be explained clearly
> without making the audience feel that they have accidentally entered a
> three-hour lecture?

Creating short-form content is also a compact product exercise: understand the
audience, find the useful idea, remove unnecessary complexity, test the
delivery, and learn from what people actually watch—not what the creator hoped
they would watch.

## Movement keeps the thinking honest

- **Basketball** teaches spacing, decision speed, and why a brilliant play is
  useless when nobody else can read it.
- **Ultimate frisbee** teaches timing, trust, and how quickly plans change once
  the disc is actually in the air.
- **Strength training** teaches the least glamorous product lesson of all:
  progress is usually a long series of ordinary repetitions wearing no special
  effects.

I like sports because feedback is immediate. The scoreboard, the missed pass,
and the final repetition are all unusually resistant to presentation slides.

## Intellectual rabbit holes

I am drawn to Chinese philosophy and intellectual history, especially:

- **Wang Yangming's unity of knowledge and action**: an idea is incomplete
  until it survives practice.
- **Mao Zedong's writing on practice and contradiction**, read as a historical
  and philosophical method for examining changing conditions, competing
  forces, and action.

My frequently revisited shelf includes the **I Ching**, **Tao Te Ching**,
**Zizhi Tongjian**, and the **Collected Works of Mao Zedong**. This is not a
reading list optimized for ideological consistency. It is a collection of
different lenses on change, incentives, judgment, power, and human action.

## The short version

I enjoy building products where finance, technology, and human behavior meet.
Away from the screen, I am usually trying to understand a game, a training
routine, a historical decision, or why a 30-second explanation took three
hours to write.

That combination keeps me practical: **understand deeply, explain simply, test
in reality, and keep iterating.**
